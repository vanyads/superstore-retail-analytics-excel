# superstore-retail-analytics-excel
Where does a retail business make money, and where does it quietly lose it? This Excel project analyzes four years of Superstore orders (2014-2017) with PivotTables and slicers to trace sales and profit, find the discount level where orders turn unprofitable, and pinpoint the products, segments, and regions that drive or drain profit.

# Project Objectives
- Analyze sales and profit performance from 2014 to 2017 to identify trends over time.
- Identify the product categories, customer segments, and regions that contribute most to or reduce overall profitability.
- Evaluate the impact of discounts on profitability and identify discount levels associated with unprofitable orders.
- Generate data-driven insights to support pricing strategies and improve business profitability.

# Tools & Technologies
Microsoft Excel — Data cleaning, KPI calculations, PivotTables, PivotCharts, and interactive dashboard development using slicers.

# Key Findings
- Discount Impact: Profitability declines as discounts increase. Orders start generating losses at 30% discount, while orders without discounts achieve the highest profit margin (0.30).
- Product Profitability: Technology was the most profitable category ($145K), driven by Copiers ($55.6K), Phones ($44.5K), and Accessories ($41.9K). Copiers alone outperformed the entire Furniture category ($18.4K).
- Seasonal Sales Trends: Sales consistently peak in September, November, and December, with November recording the highest cumulative sales ($352K). February is consistently the weakest month ($59K).
